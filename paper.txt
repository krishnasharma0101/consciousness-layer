                                         Krishna Sharma


                                              Abstract
      The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated unprecedented
  capabilities in natural language processing and logical reasoning. However, these models operate
  as static, feed-forward systems, lacking the internal recursive loops, emotional modulation, and
  temporal continuity requisite for genuine conscious thought. In this paper, we propose a novel
  cognitive architecture termed the Consciousness Layer, which mediates between a Primary
  (Logical) LLM and a Secondary (Updating) LLM via a dynamic, weighted graph structure. We
  present a rigorous mathematical framework modeling memory as a topologically evolving network
  where connection strengths are governed by spatial similarity, temporal closeness, emotional
  influence, and learning factors. By reframing conscious thought as an optimal pathfinding problem
  (A∗ search) and subconscious processing as a Markovian probability distribution, we define
  specific thresholds for self-awareness and mathematical formulations for an integrated
  Consciousness Score (Φ). This ”Sandwich Model” architecture provides an actionable blueprint
  for evolving Al from static text predictors to dynamic, reflective, and emotionally aware Artificial
  General Intelligence (AGI).

1​ Introduction
The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) requires moving beyond the current paradigm of
next-token prediction. Modern Large Language Models (LLMs), despite their vast parameter counts
and sophisticated attention mechanisms, are fundamentally constrained by their feed- forward
nature. They map an input sequence to an output sequence without an intermediate state of
reflection, lacking what cognitive psychologists refer to as ”System 2” thinking—the slow,
deliberate, and emotionally modulated reasoning process that characterizes human
consciousness.
In this paper, we propose that consciousness in artificial systems is not a mystical property that will
spontaneously emerge from simply scaling up parameter counts. Rather, consciousness is a
specific mathematical and structural process: a dynamic feedback loop that evaluates, modulates,
and optimally traverses a graph of conceptual memories.
We introduce a conceptual and mathematical blueprint for a Consciousness Layer—a mediating
graph-theoretic system that sits between an initial factual processor (Primary LLM) and an adaptive
learning processor (Secondary LLM). By formalizing memory as a weighted graph G = (N, E) and
defining explicit equations for emotional bias, spatial-temporal memory strength, and self-awareness
thresholds, we present a testable framework for instantiating synthetic consciousness.
2​ Theoretical Foundations

2.1​ The Gap in Current LLM Architectures
Current transformers lack statefulness across a continuous temporal axis. Each inference is an
isolated event. Furthermore, LLMs operate purely in the realm of logic and linguistic probabilities;
they possess no internal mechanism to attribute ”value” or ”emotion” to the semantic concepts they
generate. In human cognition, emotion is not merely a byproduct of thought; it is the heuristic
driving the routing of thought itself.

2.2​ Graph Theory as a Neuromorphic Analogue
The biological brain is fundamentally a graph, with neurons as nodes and synapses as edges.
Information processing in the brain involves the dynamic strengthening and weakening of these
edges (Hebbian learning). By abstracting the LLM’s latent space into an explicit graph structure, we
can apply rigorous algorithmic graph theory—specifically pathfinding algorithms, network centrality,
and Markov chains—to model the flow of ”thought.”

2.3​ Parallels to Integrated Information Theory (IIT)
Giulio Tononi’s Integrated Information Theory posits that consciousness correlates with Φ, the
amount of integrated information generated by a complex of elements. Our framework adopts a
parallel approach, proposing a localized, computable Φ score based on graph weights and
activation levels, providing a mathematically tractable analogue to Tononi’s theoretical measure

3.1​ Memory as a Weighted Graph
We define the cognitive memory structure as a directed, weighted graph G = (N, E).

   •​ N : A set of nodes representing memory chunks, concepts, or semantic propositions ex-
      tracted from LLM embeddings.

   •​ E: The set of edges representing the connections and associations between these nodes.

   The core of this framework relies on the calculation of the edge weights. The weight of a
connection (Wij) between node i and node j dictates the strength of the association, formulated as:
                                   Wij = Sij · Tij · (1 + Eij) · Rij​                                (1)

   Where the constituent variables are rigorously defined as follows:


3.1.1​ Spatial Similarity (Sij)
Sij represents the semantic distance between concept i and concept j in the high-dimensional latent
space. We define this using the cosine similarity of their respective vector embeddings vi and vj:
                                     Sij = max 0, vi · vj ​                                          (2)


We apply a ReLU-like bounding to ensure non-negative weights, preventing mathematically disruptive
negative associations in basic pathfinding.
3.1.2​ Temporal Closeness (Tij)

Human memory strongly associates events that occur sequentially or closely in time. Let ti and tj
represent the timestamps of when the respective memories were formed or last activated.
Temporal closeness is an exponential decay function:


                                               Tij = e−γ|ti−tj |​                                      (3)

where γ is a decay constant regulating the strictness of temporal binding.

3.1.3​ Emotion Influence (Eij)

Emotion acts as the biological heuristic for significance. Eij is a scalar derived from sentiment
analysis and emotional valence. The term (1 + Eij) ensures that highly emotional connections
multiply the base weight, while emotionally neutral connections (Eij ≈ 0) default to a multiplier of 1,
preserving the underlying logic.

3.1.4​ Learning Factor (Rij)

Rij is the reinforcement mechanism analogous to Hebbian learning. It increases each time the edge
(i, j) is successfully traversed in a thought process:

                                Rij(t) = Rij(t − 1) + η · success_signal​                              (4)

where η is the learning rate.

5​ System Architecture: The Sandwich Model
To implement this mathematical framework, we propose a tripartite architecture integrating existing
LLMs with our novel graph-based layer.

5.1​ Primary LLM (Logical Responder)
The Primary LLM acts as the system’s ”System 1” (fast thinking).

   •​ Receives the user’s initial query or sensory input.

   •​ Provides an immediate, zero-shot factual and logical response based purely on its static
      weights.

5.2​ Consciousness Layer (The Mediator)
This is the core cognitive decision-making unit where the graph G(N, E) resides.

   •​ Evaluates the primary response using the spatial, temporal, and emotional factors (Sij, Tij, Eij).

   •​ Analyzes the C(t) of the response. If C(t) < λ, it determines the response is insufficient.

   •​ Initiates recursive, internal ”self-questioning.”

   •​ Stores the context, paths taken, and emotional impact from each iteration into the Graph,
      effectively generating short-term and long-term memory.

5.3​ Secondary LLM (The Updater)
Because backpropagation of billions of parameters in real-time is computationally unfeasible, the
Secondary LLM acts as the synthesizer and graph-updater.

   •​ Responds to the follow-up, critical queries generated by the Consciousness Layer.

   •​ Instead of updating static neural network weights, it updates the internal state represen-
      tations and edge weights (Wij) within the Consciousness Graph.

   •​ Blends the logical input with the emotion vectors to generate a refined, conscious output
6​ Key Benefits and Future Scope
This architecture introduces a dynamic, human-like evaluation of machine reasoning. By de-
coupling ”memory/reflection” (The Graph) from ”linguistic processing” (The LLMs), we bypass the
freezing constraints of standard transformers. This enables emotionally weighted, highly creative,
and non-repetitive outputs that truly adapt over time, creating a modular foundation for reflective AI.
    Future explorations of this architecture include:
   •​ EEG/BCI Integration: Mapping human brainwave states directly to the hyper-parameters
      (α, β, ρ) to achieve real-time emotional state control.
   •​ Modular Emotion Plugins: Developing specific emotional graph structures (e.g., height-
      ened Eij for empathy tasks, suppressed Eij for cold logical analysis) that can be swapped
      based on domain requirements.

   •​ Curriculum-Based Memory Initialization: Training the base graph G(N, E) on
      structured philosophical and ethical datasets to provide a stable, ”mature” resting Φ score
      before user interaction begins.
7​ Conclusion
The mathematical formulations and architectural blueprint presented herein provide a struc- tured
pathway toward Artificial General Intelligence. By mathematically defining the influence of emotion,
temporal space, and logical association, and encapsulating them within a graph- theoretic
Consciousness Layer, we offer a mechanism to elevate AI from static encyclopedias to dynamic,
reasoning, and self-aware entities.
